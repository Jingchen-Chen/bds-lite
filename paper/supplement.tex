\section*{S2. Training step and comparator objectives}
\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{BDS-Lite training step and deployment}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require batch $(x,y)$, targets $y_b$ (boundary) and $\phi$ (SDF) precomputed from $y$
\State $\{f_\ell\}\gets\mathrm{Enc}(x)$;\quad $F_s\gets D_s(\{f_\ell\})$;\quad $q_s\gets P(F_s)$
\State $g\gets\sigma(W_gq_s+b_g)$;\quad $z_s\gets W_o\bigl(F_s\odot[1+\alpha(g-0.5)]\bigr)$
\State $(z_b,q_b)\gets D_b(\{f_\ell\})$ \Comment{training only}
\State $L\gets L_{\mathrm{ce}}(z_s,y)+L_{\mathrm{dice}}(z_s,y)+\lambda_{\mathrm{sdf}}L_{\mathrm{sdf}}(z_s,\phi)+\lambda_bL_{\mathrm{boundary}}(z_b,y_b)+\lambda_d\,\mathrm{SmoothL1}(q_s,\sg(q_b))$
\State backpropagate $L$ (mixed precision), clip gradient norm to 1, AdamW step
\State \textbf{Deployment:} delete $D_b$; keep Enc, $D_s$, $P$, gate, $W_o$; predict $\arg\max_k z_s$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Soft Dice.} With $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, $n$ indexing images in the batch and $u$ pixels,
\[
L_{\mathrm{dice}}=1-\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\frac{2\sum_{n,u}p_{nk}(u)y_{nk}(u)+\varepsilon}{\sum_{n,u}\left(p_{nk}(u)+y_{nk}(u)\right)+\varepsilon}.
\]

\paragraph{Surface loss (S), moved from the main text.} For image $n$ and foreground class $k$, let $\phi_{nk}(u)$ be the signed Euclidean distance of pixel $u$ to the class boundary (positive outside, negative inside; $\phi\equiv0$ if the class is absent), $s_{nk}=\max_u|\phi_{nk}(u)|$, and $v_{nk}=\mathbb{1}[s_{nk}>0]$. The surface loss is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{sdf}}=\frac{\sum_{n,k}v_{nk}\sum_u p_{nk}(u)\,\phi_{nk}(u)/s_{nk}}{HW\sum_{n,k}v_{nk}}.
\end{equation}
Because $\phi_{nk}$ changes sign at the boundary, minimizing $L_{\mathrm{sdf}}$ penalizes probability mass outside the object in proportion to its normalized distance from the boundary and rewards probability mass inside the object in proportion to its normalized depth. $L_{\mathrm{sdf}}$ is the boundary loss of Kervadec et al. (2021) with three differences: each image--class distance map is divided by its own largest absolute value $s_{nk}$, so that large and small structures contribute on one scale; its weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{sdf}}=1$ is fixed; and no schedule rebalances it against $L_{\mathrm{seg}}$. The U-Net+Boundary Loss comparator instead uses the released implementation, with unnormalized pixel distances and a schedule that shifts weight from the region loss to the boundary term during training, and the U-Net+GSL comparator normalizes a distance-weighted error and anneals from a region to a surface term (below, and S3).

\paragraph{U-Net+GSL.} $L_{\mathrm{GSL}}=\alpha_tL_{\mathrm{reg}}+(1-\alpha_t)L_{\mathrm{surf}}$ is added to $L_{\mathrm{seg}}$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{gsl}}=1$, where $L_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is cross-entropy plus squared-denominator Dice and
\[
L_{\mathrm{surf}}=1-\frac1N\sum_n\frac{\sum_kw'_{nk}\sum_u\left(D_{nk}(u)\,(1-y_{nk}(u)-p_{nk}(u))\right)^2}{\sum_kw'_{nk}\sum_uD_{nk}(u)^2},
\]
with $D_{nk}$ the exact Euclidean distance to class $k$ (image diagonal if absent) and $w'_{nk}=1/(1+(\sum_uy_{nk})^2)$. The schedule is the published step schedule: $\alpha_t=1-\lfloor(t-1)/5\rfloor/29$ for epochs $t\le145$ and $\alpha_t=0$ afterwards.

\paragraph{U-Net+Boundary Loss.} Following the official implementation, $L=w_r(t)L_{\mathrm{seg}}+w_b(t)\frac{1}{N(K-1)HW}\sum_{n,k,u}p_{nk}(u)\tilde\phi_{nk}(u)$ with unnormalized signed distances $\tilde\phi$ and the published rebalancing $w_r(1)=1$, $w_b(1)=0.01$, $w_r\leftarrow\max(0.1,w_r-0.01)$, $w_b\leftarrow w_b+0.01$ after every epoch.

\paragraph{LB-UNet fidelity and parameter accounting.} The pinned upstream source (commit 913ee68, hash verified against all nine runs) retains the paper's channel widths. Static CPU inspection gives 55,865 registered parameters for the unmodified RGB one-output model and 55,874 for our RGB two-output version; the wrapper adds none, and the output-head change adds only nine. Strict loading of all nine selected checkpoints verifies their state-dictionary shapes against the released graph with the dataset-specific input/output channels. Counting only Conv2d and BatchNorm2d gives 38,489 parameters, close to the paper's rounded 0.038\,M, but omits 15,872 learned spatial parameters, 992 custom LayerNorm parameters and 512 GroupNorm parameters. This is a possible counting-convention explanation, not evidence of how the paper counted. The class head, CE+Dice objective, morphological boundary targets and locked schedule are adaptations; the genetic-algorithm targets and published recipe were not reproduced. We therefore describe the comparator as adapted LB-UNet, not an exact reproduction.

\section*{S3. Datasets, arms, and comparator implementations}

\paragraph{Metric morphology.} Boundary F1 is computed on each class separately using dilation XOR erosion with SciPy's default four-connected cross element; a match is within two iterations of cross-element dilation (Manhattan distance), not a Euclidean-radius disk. HD95/ASSD instead use erosion-defined inner surfaces and Euclidean distance transforms on the resized grid. The KD training band uses a $3\times3$ square element and teacher predictions. Metric boundaries, reference-error contours and the KD band are therefore distinct objects.

\section*{S4. Correction of the ISIC2018 evaluation}
The first submitted version of this study evaluated ISIC2018 on \R{RV.sub.isic2018.n.all} images of which \R{RV.sub.isic2018.n.train} were training images of the evaluated models; this section documents that error and the switch to the official test set.

\paragraph{Evidence.} The original ISIC2018 U and BDS-Lite checkpoints record 226.9 optimizer steps per epoch (all six runs), equal to $1{,}815/8$ and not to $2{,}075/8$; ACDC (85) and Synapse (109) match their documented partitions. The best validation DSC in each ISIC training log equals, to six decimals, the evaluation on the 389-image validation set of the earlier split-seed-1 partition. Comparing the split manifests directly, of the 519 images of the documented validation set used for the original ISIC2018 results, 358 were in that partition's training set, 84 in its validation set, and 77 in its test set; the two partitions cover the same 2,594-image pool, and the official test set has no image identifier in common with it. Rerunning the checkpoints on the 519 images reproduces the original per-image values (maximum absolute DSC difference $4\times10^{-4}$ for U and $1.1\times10^{-3}$ for BDS-Lite). All 18 original checkpoint files are byte-identical (SHA-256) to the archive manifest of the public release. With the original aggregation, the present evaluator reproduces every ACDC and Synapse value of the original Table~2 (per seed, maximum absolute difference $2\times10^{-4}$ for DSC, IoU and Boundary F1 and 0.03\,px for HD95 and ASSD) and of the original Table~3 (mean differences within 0.015; Wilcoxon statistics identical in 9 of 10 rows), with residual differences explained by CPU versus GPU arithmetic.

\paragraph{How the error arose.} The original grid was evaluated on the 390-image test list of the earlier partition, and those per-image files are preserved. That list shares no image with these models' training or selection images. When a fault in our Boundary F1 implementation was corrected (non-empty boundaries with no overlap scored 1 instead of 0), a re-evaluation script set the ISIC2018 split to the validation set of the documented partition, on the mistaken assumption that no test set existed. This replaced the U and BDS-Lite results, and the other arms of the grid kept their 390-image evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:membership} divides the submitted paired analysis by what each image was to these models. It reproduces the submitted values exactly in its first row. The favorable HD95 and Boundary F1 differences lie in the training images. The last row uses the preserved per-image files, whose Boundary F1 comes from the historical implementation (correlation with the present one \R{LEG.r.low}--\R{LEG.r.high} on ACDC and Synapse, mean absolute difference \R{LEG.gap.low}--\R{LEG.gap.high}).

\emph{Numerical consistency.} The auxiliary gradient and its three components were obtained in separate FP32 backward passes (cuDNN convolutions with TF32 enabled), so the linearity identity $|g_{\mathrm{aux}}|\cos_{\mathrm{aux}}=\sum_k|g_k|\cos_k$ holds only up to accumulation error, and exact equivalence of the separate and combined passes is not claimed. \section*{S14. Repeatability of image-level tests}
This analysis was designed after the ten-seed results were known and uses completed runs only. For one recipe, two disjoint groups of $k$ of its ten runs are compared with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-image differences of group means, as in the prespecified families. Any significant result is therefore a difference between trained models and not between recipes. At most 2,000 splits are sampled per recipe. The table reports how often $p<0.05$ occurred. Rates stay high as $k$ grows from 3 to 5 because the image-level test measures how consistently two fixed sets of models differ across images, and this does not shrink with averaging in the way a comparison of recipes would.

\section*{S17. Exploratory external-teacher distillation (study 3)}
\paragraph{Protocol.} One protocol covered ISIC2018 (with one amendment, below) and one covered ACDC and Synapse; a single analysis script was applied to all three datasets. Each protocol was fixed before any of its teachers or students was trained, and each test set was evaluated once, after integrity checks.

\paragraph{Design.} One teacher per dataset, a U-Net with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 encoder (\path{segmentation_models_pytorch} 0.6.0; seed 100), was trained with the students' data, optimizer, schedule, budget and checkpoint rule, with the unweighted CE+Dice loss, and had to reach at least the validation Boundary F1 and DSC of D before any student was trained (all three passed). On ACDC and Synapse the single input channel is replicated to three channels; ACDC images, stored per-volume z-scored, are passed unchanged, and ISIC2018 and Synapse images receive ImageNet normalization inside the teacher. Each student equals the dataset's prespecified configuration of D except for the seed and the external-teacher distillation term $L_{\mathrm{kd}}$ of the main text, with $\lambda_{\mathrm{kd}}=1$; for two classes the loss reduces exactly to the binary loss used on ISIC2018. A student with seed $s$ starts from the same initial weights and batch order as the run of D with seed $s$, but the analysis is unpaired. The analysis used the runs of D from studies 1 and 2 (ISIC2018: seeds 1--10; ACDC and Synapse: seeds 1--3).

\section*{S20. Recipe-level standard error: a variance term counted twice (post hoc sensitivity analysis)}

\paragraph{Summary.} The recipe-level SE of the main text (Statistics) counts the run-by-unit residual variance twice, so under an additive model its intervals are conservative. This section quantifies the excess with an alternative estimator chosen after the results were known. Every reading, label and decision in the main text and in S1--S19, including the decision of study 4, uses the prespecified estimator; the alternative intervals are a sensitivity analysis.

\paragraph{The shared term.} Write the per-unit score of run $r$ of arm $a$ on test unit $u$ as $y_{aru}=\mu_a+\rho_{ar}+\upsilon_u+\gamma_{au}+\varepsilon_{aru}$, with a run effect, a unit effect common to both arms, an arm-by-unit effect and a run-by-unit residual of variance $\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}$, on test units shared by both arms. The between-run term of the main-text SE equation, $s_A^2/n_A+s_B^2/n_B$, then has expectation $\sum_a(\sigma^2_{\rho,a}+\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}/U)/n_a$, and the test-unit term has expectation $[\mathrm{Var}(\gamma_{Au}-\gamma_{Bu})+\sum_a\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}/n_a]/U$. The variance of $\Delta$ over new runs and new test units contains $\sum_a\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}/(n_aU)$ once, but SE$^2$ contains it twice, so SE$^2$ overestimates the variance by
\[
\text{shared}=\sum_a\frac{\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}}{n_a\,U}.
\]
For the linear contrasts of study 1 the same argument gives $\sigma^2_\varepsilon\sum_cw_c^2/(n_cU)$ with a residual variance pooled over cells. In three illustrative simulations under this model, the mean of SE$^2$ exceeded the empirical variance of $\Delta$, and the mean of SE$^2-\text{shared}$ matched it within Monte Carlo error.

\paragraph{Alternative estimator.} $\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,a}$ is estimated by the residual mean square of an additive run-plus-unit fit within arm $a$, pooled over cells for linear contrasts. Subtracting $k$ times the shared term from each of the two components of the SE equation gives method-of-moments estimates of the between-run and test-unit variance. Each is truncated at zero, because a variance cannot be negative, so the adjusted variance is $\max(\text{between-run}-k\cdot\text{shared},0)+\max(\text{test-unit}-k\cdot\text{shared},0)+k\cdot\text{shared}$, with $k=1$ as the central value. Without truncation, negative component estimates offset part of the other component, and in \R{RS.n.untrunc.below.shared} of the \R{RS.n.comparisons} comparisons the adjusted variance fell below the shared term itself, which under the model is a lower bound. Table~\ref{tab:s20} also gives these untruncated intervals; they are narrower because of the negative component estimates and are not a preferred result. The \R{RS.n.comparisons} comparisons are the \R{RS.n.bf1} non-pooled contrasts whose Boundary F1 rows appear in Table~\ref{tab:s20}, each evaluated on all five metrics; the ISIC2018 contrast of study 3 enters twice, as 3 vs 3 and as 3 vs 10. Of the secondary metrics, Table~\ref{tab:s20} prints only those of study 4. Degrees of freedom and reference distributions are those of the prespecified analysis, and pooled estimates of study 1 were recomputed with the adjusted per-dataset variances.

\paragraph{Size.} With the shared term removed ($k=1$), the Boundary F1 SE fell by at most \R{RS.red.isic.max}\% on ISIC2018, whose 1,000 test images make the term small, by \R{RS.red.s3}\% in study 4, and by \R{RS.red.small.min}--\R{RS.red.small.max}\% in the ACDC and Synapse contrasts of studies 1 and 3, which have 20 patients and 12 cases. The term is therefore not negligible by the criterion fixed before the first computation (every Boundary F1 SE falling by less than 5\%).

\paragraph{Stability.} At $k=1$, \R{RS.n.trunc.k1} of the \R{RS.n.comparisons} comparisons (five metrics; \R{RS.n.trunc.bf1.k1} of the \R{RS.n.bf1} Boundary F1 comparisons) had at least one component truncated at zero; at $k=0.5$ and $k=1.5$ the counts were \R{RS.n.trunc.k05} and \R{RS.n.trunc.k15}. The residual mean squares of studies 1 and 3 come from three runs per arm. Under the model the residual mean square has at least 22 degrees of freedom per arm here, so its relative sampling SD is at most about 0.30, and the range $k=0.5$ to $1.5$ covers more than $\pm1.6$ such SDs.

\paragraph{Intervals.} Table~\ref{tab:s20} gives both estimators for every Boundary F1 comparison of the main text and for the secondary metrics of study 4. Of the \R{RS.n.rows} intervals (the \R{RS.n.comparisons} comparisons above and the three pooled contrasts of study 1 in Table~\ref{tab:s20}, each on five metrics), \R{RS.n.excl.pre} exclude zero under the prespecified estimator, and \R{RS.n.excl.k05}, \R{RS.n.excl.k1} and \R{RS.n.excl.k15} with the shared term removed at $k=0.5$, 1 and 1.5. At $k=1$, \R{RS.n.flips.k1} intervals changed from including to excluding zero: \R{RS.n.flips.k1.bf1} for Boundary F1 (the ACDC D $-$ U contrast of study 1 and the pooled D+F+G $-$ D contrast) and three secondary metrics of study 3 (\R{SL.RS.flips.other}), which are not rows of Table~\ref{tab:s20}; their prespecified intervals are in Table~\ref{tab:s-kd-orig}. For pooled D+F+G $-$ D the interval was \R{RS.tr.nosurf.adj.k05.ci}, \R{RS.tr.nosurf.adj.ci} and \R{RS.tr.nosurf.adj.k15.ci} at $k=0.5$, 1 and 1.5 (prespecified: \R{A1.nosurf.boundary_f1.ci}), so whether it excludes zero depends on the size assumed for the shared term. For the primary endpoint of study 4 the interval was \R{RS.s3.adj.k05.ci}, \R{RS.s3.adj.ci} and \R{RS.s3.adj.k15.ci}.

\paragraph{Why the pooled interval is not monotone in $k$.} When both components are truncated, the adjusted variance equals its floor, $k\cdot\text{shared}$, which rises with $k$. For ACDC, the between-run component is \R{RS.pd.acdc.run}, the test-unit component \R{RS.pd.acdc.unit} and the shared term \R{RS.pd.acdc.shared} (all $\times10^{-5}$). At $k=0.5$ only the test-unit component is truncated, and the adjusted variance is the between-run component; at $k=1$ both are truncated, and it is the floor, \R{RS.pd.acdc.shared}; at $k=1.5$ the floor is \R{RS.pd.acdc.floor.k15}, above the prespecified SE$^2$ of \R{RS.pd.acdc.se2.k0}. Because ACDC carries \R{RS.pd.acdc.w.k15}--\R{RS.pd.acdc.w.k05}\% of the weight, its SE (\R{RS.pd.acdc.se.k0}, \R{RS.pd.acdc.se.k05}, \R{RS.pd.acdc.se.k1} and \R{RS.pd.acdc.se.k15} at $k=0$, 0.5, 1 and 1.5) drives the pooled SE, and the shifting weights move the pooled estimate between \R{RS.pd.pooled.est.k0} and \R{RS.pd.pooled.est.k05} (Table~\ref{tab:s20pooled}).

\paragraph{Coverage.} In a plug-in simulation (\R{RS.cov.reps} replicates for each of \R{RS.cov.n} comparisons, using their estimated variance components with negative estimates set to zero, under normal, homoscedastic errors), the 95\% intervals covered the true difference in \R{RS.cov.pre.min}--\R{RS.cov.pre.max} of replicates with the prespecified SE and in \R{RS.cov.adj.min}--\R{RS.cov.adj.max} with the shared term removed (Monte Carlo SE about 0.003; Table~\ref{tab:s20cov}).

\paragraph{Limitations and conclusion.} The residual variance rests on three runs per arm in studies 1 and 3, the model assumes additive run and unit effects with homoscedastic residuals, the degrees of freedom were not adjusted, and the alternative estimator, the truncation and the range of $k$ were chosen after the results were known. Truncation, non-monotonicity in $k$ (\R{RS.n.nonmono} comparisons) and adjusted SEs above the prespecified SE at $k=1.5$ (\R{RS.n.above.pre.k15} comparisons) show that the variance components are poorly determined. The prespecified estimator therefore remains the basis of inference; it is conservative under the model, and no reading rests on the adjusted intervals.

\section*{S22. Post hoc analyses: reference estimators, error location, and the D mean across studies}
All analyses in this section were designed after the results of study 4 were known.

\paragraph{Reference estimators.} The prespecified SE adds a between-run term and a test-unit term (main-text Methods), each of which contains the run-by-unit residual (S20). For the contrasts of studies 4 and 5 we fitted a crossed random-effects model, $y_{aru}=\mu_a+r_{ar}+p_u+(ap)_{au}+e_{aru}$, with runs nested in arms and patients crossed with arms, by the method of moments for this balanced design: $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\Delta)=\mathrm{MS}_{\mathrm{run},A}/(n_AU)+\mathrm{MS}_{\mathrm{run},B}/(n_BU)+\max\{0,\,s_d^2-\hat\sigma_e^2(1/n_A+1/n_B)\}/U$, with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. It counts the residual once and agrees with the S20 adjustment at $k=1$ up to the degrees of freedom. A hierarchical bootstrap (10{,}000 draws; patients resampled once per draw for both arms, runs resampled within each arm) is shown as a second reference; resampling both levels can add residual variance under the crossed model, but does not guarantee coverage under misspecification. Table~\ref{tab:s-ref} lists all three. They change no prespecified reading.

\section*{S23. Frozen teachers on the official ACDC patients (post hoc, descriptive)}
\paragraph{Status.} This evaluation was added after every student result on patients 101--150 had been examined. It is inference only, post hoc and descriptive, and it changes no prespecified reading. Its interpretation rule was fixed before the teacher inference reported here; the external teacher's earlier one-time evaluation on these patients had been seen before then, and the new predictions are pixel-identical to it.

\paragraph{Method.} Each frozen teacher (the external teacher of D+KD-band, D+KD-full and D+KD-band$_{\mathrm{aug}}$, and the compact teacher of D+KD-band$_{\mathrm{cmp}}$) was evaluated with the student evaluator and the patient-level aggregation of the main text. Before any teacher was evaluated, all \R{TE.recomputed} student runs were recomputed from their saved predictions and equalled their saved per-case files, and \R{TE.hashes} artifact hashes matched. Teacher minus group is the teacher's patient score minus the group's per-patient mean over runs, averaged over the \R{OT.patients} patients, with a 95\% percentile bootstrap over patients (\R{TE.draws} draws). These intervals are conditional on the evaluated runs, are not the run-level estimator of the main text, and are not tests.

\paragraph{Result.} The external teacher scored \R{TE.ext.bf1} in Boundary F1, and none of the \R{TE.runs} student runs exceeded it (Table~\ref{tab:s-teacher-groups}); the compact teacher scored \R{TE.cmp.bf1}, and \R{TE.cmp.D.above} of \R{TE.cmp.D.runs} D runs scored higher. The prespecified rule compared the external teacher with D$_{\mathrm{aug}}$ (teacher minus D$_{\mathrm{aug}}$ \R{TE.ext.Daug.delta}, \R{TE.ext.Daug.text}): outcome \R{TE.outcome}, ``This explanation is not supported'', the explanation being that augmented students score above the teacher. A further check, defined after these results: without augmentation, D+KD-band closed \R{TE.prop.share}\% of the external teacher's lead over D (confirmatory contrast divided by teacher minus D); the same share of the teacher's lead over D$_{\mathrm{aug}}$ gives \R{TE.prop.expected}, just above the upper bound \R{TE.prop.upper} of the augmented contrast's 95\% interval. The check uses point estimates only and ignores the uncertainty of the confirmatory gain, of both teacher leads and of the interval, so it narrowly excludes proportional transfer at face value and tests nothing. One realization of each teacher was evaluated; the result does not explain the distillation effect or establish teacher quality as its cause.

\section*{S24. Further post hoc analyses: interaction, pooling, run-to-run variation, missed structures}
\paragraph{Status.} The analyses of this section were added after every result of the article was known. They use saved per-case evaluation outputs only; no model was trained or executed. They are post hoc and exploratory and change no prespecified estimate, decision or reading.

\paragraph{Augmentation-by-distillation interaction.} On the official ACDC patients 101--150, $I=(\bar m_{\mathrm{KD}}-\bar m_{\mathrm{D}})-(\bar m_{\mathrm{KD,aug}}-\bar m_{\mathrm{D,aug}})$ is the difference between the replication contrast (study 4) and the contrast under augmentation (S21). All four arms were scored on the same 50 patients, so the two contrasts share patient effects, and adding their squared standard errors would count the patient term of each contrast separately. Three standard errors are therefore reported. \emph{Prespecified form:} the estimator of the main text extended to a four-arm linear contrast, $\mathrm{SE}^2=\sum_a s_a^2/n_a+\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_u(dd_u)/U$, where $dd_u$ is the per-patient double difference of arm means and the degrees of freedom are the Welch--Satterthwaite value of the four between-run terms (for Boundary F1, \R{RV.int.all.boundary_f1.df}). \emph{Crossed model:} the method-of-moments estimate of S22 for four arms, $\sum_a\mathrm{MS}_{\mathrm{run},a}/(n_aU)+\max\{0,\,s_{dd}^2-\hat\sigma_e^2\sum_a 1/n_a\}/U$, which counts the run-by-patient residual once (S20). \emph{Bootstrap:} 10{,}000 draws in which patients are resampled once and shared by the four arms and runs are resampled within each arm. For Boundary F1 the prespecified-form standard error is \R{RV.int.all.boundary_f1.se}; the sum of the two contrasts' squared standard errors gives \R{RV.int.all.boundary_f1.se.naive}, so the treatment of the shared patients changes little here, because the between-run terms dominate. The unaugmented arms have 17 runs and the augmented arms 10; the lower block of Table~\ref{tab:s-review-interaction} restricts all four arms to seeds 201--210. Seeds are not paired in any estimate.
